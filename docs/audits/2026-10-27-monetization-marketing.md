## Verdict

**Yes to the half that matters, with conditions on the other half.**

1. **Making every bank free is the right call, and it is close to a no-regret move.** Today two thirds of the product is invisible to a free user, nothing is purchasable (no RevenueCat keys, no store products, no DNS), and the catalogue has grown to 24 paid SKUs at $4.99 each with no bundle. "Every bank is free" is a launch headline; "Easy is free, the rest is $4.99 per bank" is a reason to leave.
2. **Ads will not be meaningful revenue at any scale Syntactical can reach in the next year.** On mobile, a US user who plays eight rounds a month is worth roughly $0.03 to $0.12 a month in styled native ads (my calculation, assumptions below). On the web, developer ad blocking (about 72% in the two surveys that measure it) cuts whatever a network pays by nearly three quarters, and the networks that fit the brand have traffic floors the site does not meet yet. Ads earn their place as the thing that gives the ad-free purchase a reason to exist, and as inventory that a direct sponsor can buy later.
3. **The revenue line is the ad-free tier, not the ads.** A Pro year at $19.99, or a lifetime at $39.99, nets more than the ads from the same user would return in many years. Treat the ad as a light, between-rounds nudge toward Pro, not as the business. Pro is sold as annual or lifetime, never monthly (the owner's choice on 2026-10-27; see "Alternatives considered").
4. **Every ad is a card in the app's own design, never a network-rendered unit.** On mobile that is AdMob's native format, where the app renders the headline, body, icon, and call to action in its own component and the SDK adds only the AdChoices icon; on the web it is a text ad from a developer network or a direct sponsor rendered into the same card. This rules out AdSense, which serves its own iframe, and it costs some eCPM against full-screen interstitials, which were small anyway.
5. **Keep the privacy posture.** Non-personalised ads only, no App Tracking Transparency prompt, and on the web a network that sets no cookies. That keeps the "no tracking, no advertising identifier" story that the privacy policy, labels, and review notes are built on.
6. **Move the web app off GitHub Pages to Cloudflare Pages as a prerequisite.** Not because Pages forbids the model, but because the owner wants web ad inventory and the freedom that comes with a host that has no commercial-use clause, no bandwidth soft limit on static assets, and edge logic if consent ever needs it. DNS for `syntactical.dev` is already on Cloudflare.

If the owner wants a one-line version: free all content, sell Pro as an annual or a lifetime purchase, show one sponsored card in the app's own style on the results screen and in the track list, on the web and on mobile, with a house Pro card in the slot until a network or sponsor fills it, and measure before adding anything else.

## The marketing case for the switch

**The paid-bank model hides the product.** A new user sees ten to twelve Easy banks and 24 locked tiles. The locked tiles cannot be previewed, the paywall copy is "Unlock Medium", and the price is per bank. The strongest content (Hard banks on runtime semantics, the security tracks) is exactly what a developer would share or screenshot, and it is behind the gate. Free content is the acquisition channel for an app with no marketing budget; gating two thirds of it starves the channel.

**24 SKUs is a pricing problem on its own.** Each bank is a separate purchase decision, a separate App Store Connect and Play Console product, and a separate RevenueCat package. There is no bundle, no "all tracks", and no price anchor. A user who likes Python Medium has to decide again for Python Hard, then again for TypeScript. The per-bank model also fights the product's own roadmap: every new track adds two more products to configure and two more purchase decisions.

**"Pay to remove ads" is a message developers already understand.** It asks for one decision, it does not withhold content, and it reads as fair. It is also one product to configure instead of 24, which removes most of the store-setup work in `docs/store/in-app-purchases.md`.

**The free-everything headline works on every channel at once.** The listing subtitle, the Hacker News post, the README, and a tweet all get the same line: twelve tracks, 3,600 questions, all free. The current listing draft cannot say that and spends its "What you get" section explaining which banks cost money.

## The case against, and what it costs

**Developers block ads.** The Stack Overflow 2018 survey put regular ad-blocker use among developers at 72%, and a 2024 Ghostery survey of US adults put programmers at the same 72%. That is the web figure; in-app mobile ads are not blocked by browser extensions, though DNS-level blockers catch some. Plan on web ad revenue being a rounding error and on mobile ad revenue being reduced by some share of users who block at the network level.

**Ad eCPMs are small and the audience is small.** Vendor benchmarks for US iOS put rewarded video at $10 to $30, interstitials at $3 to $15, and banners at $0.50 to $2.50 per thousand impressions, with Android lower and non-US traffic lower again. Developer-focused web networks (EthicalAds, Carbon) pay $2.25 to $2.75 per thousand pageviews and want 50,000 monthly pageviews before they take a site. At the scale a side project reaches in its first year, these multiply out to tens or low hundreds of dollars a month. The unit-economics section below shows the arithmetic.

**Ads strain the brand.** The aesthetic brief was tactical, dark, no cartoonish anything. A mediated banner from a casual-game advertiser under a question about Postgres isolation levels is the opposite of that. The owner's answer is to render every ad inside the app's own card component (the `SelectionCard` idiom with a "Sponsored" eyebrow), which the native ad formats allow and which keeps the advertiser's creative to a headline, a line of body text, a small icon, and a button. An ad during a keyboard-driven round still breaks the "high velocity" promise the product is built on, so placement stays off the question card. Styled cards and placement discipline are the mitigation, and together they cost some revenue against full-screen formats.

**Ads change the privacy story that the store copy rests on.** Adding an ad SDK means an "Advertising Data" row in the privacy labels, a consent flow for EEA users (Google's User Messaging Platform or an equivalent), SKAdNetwork entries on iOS, and, if personalised ads are on, the ATT prompt and "Used for tracking: Yes". The current labels, policy, and review notes all say the opposite. Non-personalised ads avoid ATT and the tracking label, at lower eCPM.

**Web ads need a network that will take the site, and the brand-matched requirement leaves only two.** The web app is a client-rendered single-page export (`web.output: 'single'` in `app.config.ts`) with no crawlable text. AdSense is the only network that takes a brand-new site, and it is excluded here: it renders its own iframe, so it cannot sit inside a styled card, and it brings cookie consent banners and a tracking disclosure. EthicalAds and Carbon render a text ad into a container the site styles, set no cookies, and pay about $2.50 per thousand pageviews, but EthicalAds asks for 50,000 monthly pageviews first and Carbon is curated. So at launch the web slot shows a house Pro card or a direct sponsor the owner sells, and a network fills it once the site qualifies. Qualifying also means giving the site indexable pages, which serves search acquisition before it serves ads.

**Free content becomes scrapable.** The paid banks live in a private repo partly for protection. Once they are free they will be downloaded. This matters less than it looks: the questions come from a model-assisted pipeline that can regenerate them, and the defensible part of the product is the pipeline, the oracle validation, the review scheduling, and the UX, not any one bank.

### The hidden cost that turns out to be small

"Ask about this card" calls Claude Haiku 5.5, listed at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens. A user who hits the current cap of 20 asks a day, at roughly 1,500 input and 300 output tokens per ask, costs about $0.006 a day, or under $0.20 a month, and almost nobody hits the cap. The weekly study note is one short call a week. Neither needs to stay behind a purchase for cost reasons. They can stay behind Pro for conversion reasons, which is a different and better argument: "remove ads" alone converts weakly when the ads are light, so Pro needs a second benefit.

## Audience and channel reality

**Mobile: AdMob native ads in the app's own card.** AdMob's native format hands the app the ad's assets (headline, body, icon, call to action, optional media) and the app renders them in its own component; the SDK adds the AdChoices icon in a corner the app chooses. Google's rules for that layout: an "Ad" or "Sponsored" label of at least 15 points at the top of the card, rendered by the app; the AdChoices icon left visible against the background; every asset inside the native ad view; a minimum card size of 32 points each way; and, if video is ever accepted, a media view of at least 120 points each way (the recommendation here is text and icon only, no media view, which also keeps the card quiet). Full-screen native is permitted, so a sponsored card between rounds in the existing `DialogShell` is allowed, though the results screen is the better home. `react-native-google-mobile-ads` is the library in an Expo app; it needs a development build rather than Expo Go, which the project already does through EAS, its native-ad API and config plugin need version checking against the project's Expo SDK at implementation time, and it brings the UMP consent flow, SKAdNetwork identifiers, and label updates.

**Web: a styled text ad from a non-tracking network or a direct sponsor, on a host that allows it.** The EthicalAds client renders a text ad (up to 100 characters, optionally a small image) into a container the site owns and styles with its own CSS, sets no cookies, and needs no consent banner; Carbon works the same way by invitation. Both fit the card. AdSense does not: it serves an iframe the site cannot restyle and requires a certified consent platform for EEA and UK visitors. Until the site reaches the EthicalAds floor of 50,000 monthly pageviews, the slot shows a house Pro card or a sponsor the owner sells directly, which is how most developer sites start. Two web prerequisites follow. Move hosting from GitHub Pages to Cloudflare Pages: the DNS is already there, the free tier has no commercial-use clause and no bandwidth soft limit on static assets, and the deploy is the same static directory with a different workflow step (Vercel's free tier forbids commercial use and is the one to avoid). Switch the Expo export from the single-page output to static output and add crawlable pages with real text, such as one per track and one per question explanation, so the site is indexable; that is a search-acquisition channel the app lacks today, and the thing a network's review looks for.

**Developers reward restraint and punish the opposite in public.** This audience writes the reviews, the Hacker News comments, and the "why I uninstalled" posts. One sponsored card on the results screen and one in the track list, in the app's own typography, non-personalised, with a clear "Sponsored" label and a one-tap path to remove them, is defensible and reads as the sponsor model developer sites already use. Anything heavier risks the rating and the word of mouth that is the only acquisition channel.

## Comparables

- **Duolingo** is the pattern: free with ads, a subscription that removes ads and adds perks. Advertising was about 8% of revenue in early 2025 (my calculation from reported quarterly figures), the 10-K notes ads carry lower margins than subscriptions, and paid subscribers were about 10 million against a monthly active base in the low hundreds of millions. Even the best-run ad-plus-subscription learning app earns its money from the subscription.
- **Mimo, Sololearn, Enki, Brilliant** all run freemium with a subscription; Sololearn has shown ads to free users. None gates content per module the way Syntactical does today.
- **Stack Overflow, daily.dev, documentation sites on Carbon or EthicalAds** show that developer ads work when they are relevant and unobtrusive, and that the economics only make sense at very large pageview counts.

## Recommended shape

1. **All 36 banks free.** Serve them the same way the free banks are served now (static host with hash verification), which also removes the private-repo deploy coupling and the `PAID_CONTENT_DIR` machinery.
2. **Pro as two products: an annual subscription at about $19.99 and a lifetime non-consumable at about $39.99, no monthly.** Both remove ads, raise the Ask daily limit, and are the home for future perks, including any expansion packs. Annual gives the recurring revenue that funds new tracks; lifetime keeps the subscription-averse developer, and pricing it at about two annuals is the usual ratio. The lifetime product reuses the non-consumable path already written; the annual needs the server to grant on `INITIAL_PURCHASE` and `RENEWAL` and revoke on `EXPIRATION`, which `server/src/constants/revenueCatEvents.ts` does not list today (it grants only on `NON_RENEWING_PURCHASE`). When an annual lapses the user returns to the free tier with ads; say so plainly on the paywall.
3. **Ads: one `SponsoredCard` component, used on the results screen and in the track list, on web and mobile.** It is the `SelectionCard` idiom with a "Sponsored" eyebrow, a headline, one line of body text, a small icon, and one button, and nothing else. Never mid-round and never on the question card. On mobile it is fed by AdMob native assets, non-personalised; on the web by the EthicalAds client, a direct sponsor, or the house Pro card. Frequency-cap the results-screen card (for example, not more than one per three rounds, and none in the first session) so the first impression of the app is the product, not the ad.
4. **Ask free for everyone at a lower daily cap, Pro at the current cap.** Costs cents, converts better than "remove ads" alone, and lets guests see the feature. Keep the study note sign-in-only as it is.
5. **Web: the same card, filled in this order.** House Pro card at launch, a direct sponsor when one can be sold, EthicalAds once the site passes 50,000 monthly pageviews. AdSense never, for the brand and consent reasons above.
6. **Privacy posture: unchanged in spirit.** No ATT, no personalised ads, UMP consent in the EEA and UK on mobile, a cookie-free network on the web, labels and policy updated to match.
7. **Prerequisites on the web: Cloudflare Pages and static output.** Move the static export to Cloudflare Pages (the DNS is already there), switch `web.output` from `single` to `static`, and add crawlable track and explanation pages. The hosting move is about a day; the static pages are their own slice and pay for themselves in search traffic.

What I would not do: run AdSense anywhere (it cannot be restyled and brings cookie consent), run AdMob on the web through a web view, run rewarded video to unlock content (it recreates the gate the switch removes), sell a monthly plan, or put any ad on the question card.

## Alternatives considered

Five shapes were weighed against the recommendation above. The owner chose annual plus lifetime, ads on the web, a hosting move, and brand-styled cards on 2026-10-27.

**An ad-free web.** The first draft's position: the web is the funnel, developer ad blocking makes web inventory nearly worthless, and GitHub Pages could stay. Set aside because the owner wants web inventory and the freedom of a host without a commercial-use clause. The revenue arithmetic did not change; what changed is that the slot exists from day one as a house or sponsor card, and the hosting move became a prerequisite.

**Network-rendered formats (full-screen interstitials, standard banners, AdSense display).** Set aside for the brand. They pay more per impression (US iOS interstitials at $3 to $15 versus a native card at perhaps $1 to $5) but they put a casual-game creative under a question about runtime semantics. The native formats keep the advertiser's creative to text and an icon inside the app's own card, and the difference in revenue is small in absolute terms at any scale in the tables above.

**Keep the paid Medium and Hard banks, add ads to the free tier, and sell further banks as expansion packs.** Rejected. A free user would get the degraded experience (ads) and the withheld product (two thirds of the banks) at once, which compounds the discoverability problem the switch exists to fix, and a developer audience reads "ads plus paid unlocks" in one app as greedy. Expansion packs are sound only when they are additive and distinct, a new track or a themed bundle rather than the Hard tier of a track the user already started, and only once the free base is complete. If packs come later, sell them as whole-track bundles, include them in Pro, and keep every difficulty of every core track free.

**Pro as a monthly subscription.** Rejected. At $2.99 a month (about $2.54 net) a subscriber passes a $9.99 one-time purchase at roughly three and a half months of retention, and quiz usage is bursty, so that retention is unproven. The stronger objection is the audience: developers are the most subscription-averse segment for small tools, "pay monthly to remove ads" is the version they mock, and ads reappearing on lapse each month feels punitive. Some monthly revenue would come from people who forget to cancel, which this audience notices and reviews. Annual keeps the recurring revenue without the monthly optics.

**Pro as a single one-time purchase at $9.99 to $14.99.** This was the first draft's recommendation and remains defensible: it fits the brand, reuses the non-consumable code, and is the simplest store setup. It was set aside because it caps lifetime value at one payment while content, hosting, and the Ask model are ongoing costs, and because adding an annual tier later with lifetime buyers grandfathered is easy, whereas moving from a subscription back to one-time is not. The lifetime product in the chosen shape preserves this option for the buyer.

## Decision gates and what to measure

Ads are the reversible part of this decision and the part that depends on scale. The sequence that de-risks it:

1. **Before anything else, turn on PostHog** (the key is the only missing piece) so the next decisions have data. Add two events: `ad_shown` and `pro_purchased`, and keep `round_completed` as the denominator.
2. **Ship free-all-content, Pro, the hosting move, and the `SponsoredCard` showing the house Pro card first; the ad SDKs second.** The free-content change, the two Pro products, Cloudflare Pages, and the card with house content are good under either outcome and let the layout settle before a network fills it. Adding AdMob is a separate PR that can wait for the first four to eight weeks of data.
3. **Gates for turning AdMob on (mobile):** at least 1,000 monthly active mobile users, a median of 4 or more completed rounds per active user per month, and the UMP, label, and policy updates done. Below that, network ads cost more in brand and review risk than they return.
4. **Gates for a web network:** 50,000 monthly pageviews on the static pages (the EthicalAds floor) and the crawlable pages live. A direct sponsor has no gate beyond finding one; the card is ready for it from launch.
5. **Watch for:** rating drops in the two weeks after ads ship, `ad_shown` to `round_started` ratio (a user who stops after the first ad is the signal to loosen the frequency cap), Pro conversion from the ad-free prompt versus from the Ask limit prompt, the annual-to-lifetime split among buyers, and, from month 13, the annual renewal rate.
